Atlantic Pollock

Field observations

One of the most abundant fish around Appledore Island. Seen in all life stages throughout summer. Adult pollock almost always solitary - once in a pair. Juveniles appear from medium-sized to massive schools (>1000 in one frame). Showed preference for exposed side of the island. Schools appeared several times alongside changes in current (possible evidence of schooling behavior in response to eddying of tide and current collecting plankton?). Frequent bycatch during mackerel fishing trips. Tended to school higher in the water column than cod. Distinguishable from cod due to two-tone countershading rather than marbling. Slightly more torpedo shaped body and differences in tail shape visible in hand/good footage. Distinctive solid lateral line in adults.

Where to find them

How to read the maps
  • Remote underwater video site, higher abundance
  • Remote underwater video site, seen infrequently
  • Successful trap or seine effort (for seine species, blue diamonds also mark reliable tern foraging)
  • Successful rod and reel catch
  • Red marks on the dark bathymetry maps show bait-fishing catch locations
